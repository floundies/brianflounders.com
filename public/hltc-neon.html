Two nighttime expressions of the inherited Lansdowne L—followed by the same identity with the electricity turned off. The building dates to 1927; its nonprofit steward dates to 2006.

The octagonal frame comes from the geometry of the theater’s chandelier. Its luminous blue or gold lines recall the Lansdowne’s exterior neon, while the ring of warm dots comes from the bulbs of the marquee. We kept the original script L because it carries the memory of the preservation campaign, then separated 1927 for the building from 2006 for HLTC. The result is intended to feel born from this theater—not applied to it—and to work both as a living nighttime sign and as a disciplined flat mark in print.

The shared thought process

A system for a theater—and its steward.

This page records the route, not only the destination. The animated octagons above remain the current favorite. The studies below preserve the useful questions we tested together: inheritance versus reinvention, the building versus the nonprofit, night versus print, and campaign voice versus permanent identity.

Working note retained from the development processThe inherited L is nudged eight units left of mathematical center to compensate for its heavy lower swash. Both versions use the requested corporate lockup and a restrained animated neon hum. These remain explorations—not Board Review—until you say otherwise.
01 · Inheritance

Keep the real L.

The original script letter is the visual memory of the old Save the Lansdowne identity. Its silhouette was redrawn as a smooth vector, not replaced with a typeface.

02 · Optical balance

Trust the eye.

The low swash makes mathematical centering look wrong. Every primary mark compensates by shifting the L eight units left.

03 · Two dates

1927 / 2006.

The building carries 1927. Historic Lansdowne Theater Corporation carries 2006. The hierarchy honors both without rewriting either history.

04 · Stewardship

Protect and preserve.

“Save” was the campaign that got us here. “Protect,” “preserved by,” and “keep the lights on” describe the work that continues.

Idea 03

The working lockup

The inherited L without glow, shadow, or campaign language. This asks whether the silhouette and formal name can carry a durable institutional identity on their own.

Idea 04

The preservation medallion

A round, sign-like companion to the octagon. The theater’s 1927 date lives in the mark; HLTC’s 2006 date remains in the stewardship line.

Idea 05

The Lobby Star

An eight-point architectural motif drawn from the building’s ornament, tested as a radiant field behind the inherited L. It is celebratory, compact, and unmistakably different from the neon framework.
