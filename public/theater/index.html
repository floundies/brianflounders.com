A New Chapter

The doors are open.
The work continues.

After a $21 million restoration and 38 years dark, the Lansdowne reopened in 2025 as a live performance destination. The building was saved; caring for it is the work that continues.

HLTC owns and preserves the landmark. BRE Presents operates the venue and books its shows. One theater, two organizations, and two very different reasons to visit the links below.

We steward the building.

The Historic Lansdowne Theater Corporation is the 501(c)(3) nonprofit that owns this theater. We don’t book the bands and we don’t sell a single ticket. We raise the money that keeps a century-old landmark standing, beautiful, and here for decades to come — and that work never ends.

Then & Now

Same seats. Same view.
Ninety-nine years apart.

Drag the handle to travel between 1927 and today.

1927 2026
1927
Grand Opening
Architect William Harold Lee designs the Lansdowne Theater in the Spanish Revival style. It opens June 1, 1927 as a first-run movie house with a Kimball pipe organ — the last theater organ installed in the Philadelphia region. Miss Lansdowne flies overhead in a biplane, showering attendees with roses.
July 1987
The Fire
An electrical fire in an adjacent basement during a showing of Beverly Hills Cop II forces the theater to close. The auditorium survives — but the doors stay shut for 38 years.
2006
HLTC Founded
A small group of community members refuses to let the building die. The Historic Lansdowne Theater Corporation forms and purchases the theater in 2007 — weeks before it would have become a warehouse.
August 22, 2025
Reopening Night
After 38 years of silence, the Lansdowne opens its doors with Chazz Palminteri’s “A Bronx Tale.” 1,280 seats. Sold out. A community reborn.

The Building

Spanish Revival. Hollywood Moorish.
One of a kind.

Every detail — from the ornate plasterwork to the tiled fountains — was designed to transport audiences before the show even started. Nearly a century later, it still does.
