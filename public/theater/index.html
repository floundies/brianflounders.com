Who We Are

The stewards of a
Delaware County landmark

The Historic Lansdowne Theater Corporation is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization and the steward of one of Delaware County’s most beloved landmarks. Since 2006, we have led the $21 million restoration of the 1927 Lansdowne Theater — transforming a fire-damaged ruin into the premier concert venue it is today.

“We don’t book the bands. We don’t sell the tickets. We preserve the building.”

The Restoration

From ruin to revival

An 18-year, $21 million labor of love. What started as a small group of volunteers refusing to let a building die became one of the most significant historic preservation projects in the Philadelphia region.

1927
Grand Opening
Architect William Harold Lee designs the Lansdowne Theater in the Spanish Revival style. It opens as a first-run movie house with a Kimball pipe organ and 1,400 seats. Miss Lansdowne flies overhead in a biplane showering attendees with roses.
1965 – 1986
The Sara Gail Era
“I didn’t buy this old movie house to make a lot of money.” Sara Gail keeps the theater alive with $2 adult tickets while multiplexes close single-screen cinemas across the country.
July 1987
The Fire
An electrical fire in an adjacent basement during a showing of Beverly Hills Cop II forces the theater to close. The building does not burn — but it will not reopen for 38 years.
2006
HLTC Founded
The Historic Lansdowne Theater Corporation is formed. A small group of community members refuses to let the building die. The long road to restoration begins.
2012
Silver Screen Cameo
The theater exterior appears in Silver Linings Playbook starring Bradley Cooper and Jennifer Lawrence. The restored marquee lights up for the first time since 1987.
2019
BRE Partnership
HLTC secures BRE Presents as the operating tenant. With a programming partner in place, the full $18M+ rehabilitation begins in earnest.
August 22, 2025
Reopening Night
After 38 years of silence, the Lansdowne Theater opens its doors with Chazz Palminteri’s one-man show “A Bronx Tale.” 1,260 seats. Sold out. A community reborn.

The Building

Spanish Revival.
Hollywood Moorish.
One of a kind.

Designed by architect William Harold Lee and opened in 1927, the Lansdowne Theater is a masterwork of the movie palace era. Every detail — from the ornate plasterwork to the Moorish arches — was designed to transport audiences before the show even started.

I
244-Socket Chandelier
The 12-foot-wide centerpiece was restored by Grand Light, who also replicated over 30 original luminaires throughout the theater.
II
Hand-Painted Ceiling
The original auditorium ceiling — nearly 100 years old — has been carefully preserved and restored to its original grandeur.
III
Original Tapestries
Century-old tapestries above the balconies were taken down, professionally cleaned, mended, backed, and rehung.
IV
Moorish Arches & Fountains
Tile-clad fountains and ornamental arches reflect the Spanish Baroque influence that makes this theater unique in the region.
V
Restored Marquee
The 1920s-style marquee with neon blue letters and red show titles lights up Lansdowne Avenue once again.
VI
Patron Lounge
A new 1,500 square foot patron lounge, lobby bar, and expanded concession areas bring modern amenities to the historic space.

Our Communications Strategy

  1. Triumph, not rescue. The theater has been saved. The story is now about sustaining something extraordinary.
  2. Building, not bands. HLTC is the landlord, not the promoter. We focus on the building, its history, and its future.
  3. Community ownership. This theater belongs to Lansdowne. Every donor, volunteer, and visitor is part of the story.
  4. Ongoing need. The restoration is complete, but a century-old building requires constant investment.
